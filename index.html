How to Read the Map Beside Each Clip

The two panels on the right of every card are what the scorer worked from. Neither one is generated: the frames are the clip's own, and the faces are the lip feature bank, built once from English video.

(a) Original video
The clip's frames in order, with the words the actor spoke laid along the same timeline.
(b) The lip feature bank, twice
The mouth shapes the lip feature bank holds, placed in the encoder's 2-D space. Left is the candidate TextSync scored best, right is the one it scored worst — the same video, two lines asking for different mouths.
Red numbers & diamonds
Red numbers are the order of mouth shapes a line asks for. Diamonds are the video's own frames, identical in both panels.
Diamond fill
How well the mouth a line asks for at that frame matches what the frame looks like. The upper figure ranks it among the 22 shapes; the lower one marks matched or not, and fades the frames where both lines get the same verdict — what is left is where the two lines differ.

Data and Terms

The talking-head clips are AI-generated with LTX-2.3 and a TalkVid audio-to-video LoRA; the people on screen are synthetic and are not real individuals. The LoRA weights are not distributed here. The clips are shown for illustration and are not released as a dataset.

The single line of dialogue quoted on each card comes from the Heroes corpus (Öktem et al., 2018) and is used for research and educational purposes. No original video, audio, or full script is included or redistributed — the dubbed speech is synthesized with Kokoro-82M (Apache-2.0) and is not the original recording.

The prototype faces in the lip feature bank maps are small crops from LRS3-TED, shown for illustration under its non-commercial research terms, at the scale used in the paper’s figures.
